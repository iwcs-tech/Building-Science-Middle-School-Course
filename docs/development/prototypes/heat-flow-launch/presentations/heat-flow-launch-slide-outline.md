# Heat-Flow Launch Slide Outline

_Draft presentation content. Build an editable slide deck only if it improves delivery for the teacher._

Use simple diagrams, large text, and descriptive alt text. Do not use color as the only way to distinguish warmer and cooler locations or the three mechanisms.

## Lesson 1: Notice a Heat-Flow Pattern

| Slide | Purpose | Suggested content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Title | Launch the sequence. | “How does thermal energy move through a building?” Include the driving question. |
| 2. Notice and wonder | Invite initial observations. | Show the fictional Classroom Alder diagram without explanations. Ask: “What do you notice? What do you wonder?” |
| 3. Anchor idea | Establish the first model rule. | “Thermal energy tends to move from warmer places toward cooler places.” Include one simple warm-to-cool arrow. |
| 4. Your job today | Clarify the product. | “Use evidence to draw a first model. Models can improve.” Show the three requirements: arrows, temperatures, evidence note. |

## Lesson 2: Explain the Mechanisms

| Slide | Purpose | Suggested content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5. Revisit the model | Connect to Lesson 1. | Show an anonymous example arrow plus the question: “What kind of heat transfer might this be?” |
| 6. Conduction | Introduce direct-contact transfer. | Definition plus a simplified wall-layer or window-frame diagram. |
| 7. Convection | Introduce transfer through moving air. | Definition plus an air-loop diagram. Use the wording “warm air can rise,” not “heat rises.” |
| 8. Radiation | Introduce transfer by waves. | Definition plus sunlight crossing a room to warm a desk. |
| 9. Sort directions | Set up the team task. | “Choose the dominant mechanism in each simplified card. Defend your choice with evidence. Real systems can involve more than one.” |

## Lesson 3: Apply a Design Choice

| Slide | Purpose | Suggested content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10. Design brief | Frame the fictional problem. | Show Classroom Alder, the warm desk, and the four decision factors: desk temperature, daylight, cooling-demand index, cost. |
| 11. Model outputs | Display the options clearly. | Recreate the two-option data table. Add: “These are fictional model outputs, not a repair estimate.” |
| 12. Make a claim | Support the written explanation. | Sentence frame: “I recommend Option __ because __. The evidence is __. One tradeoff is __. One limitation is __.” |
| 13. Reusable routine | End by reinforcing the thread. | Five steps: temperature difference; path; mechanism; evidence; response/tradeoff. |

## Accessibility Check Before Use

- Every diagram has descriptive alt text.
- Temperature values and categories appear in text, not color alone.
- Definitions are visible before students begin the sort.
- Slides use large, readable fonts and sufficient contrast.
