# Lesson 3 Teacher Plan: Apply Evidence to a Design Choice

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Which fictional design option best meets the stated criteria and constraints?

## Learning Target

I can compare two design options, use evidence to make a claim, and explain a tradeoff and limitation.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Design Choice Analysis student page](../student/lesson-03-design-choice-analysis.md).
- Display Slides 9–12 from the [launch slide outline](../presentations/heat-flow-launch-slide-outline.md).
- Decide whether students will work independently after a brief team comparison or create one group submission.

## Materials

- Lesson 3 student page
- Students’ revised Lesson 2 models
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared decision matrix

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit the five-step explanation routine. Ask students to predict which mechanism is most relevant when afternoon sunlight warms a desk. | Recall radiation and the need for evidence. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the fictional design brief, criteria, constraints, and data table. Explain that the figures are modeled instructional data, not a real repair estimate. | Identify the task and underline the criteria. |
| 10–18 min | Model scoring one row in the decision matrix without choosing a final option. Ask, “What does this number tell us, and what does it not tell us?” | Begin comparing the options. |
| 18–28 min | Students complete the matrix, choose an option, and identify a tradeoff. Circulate with questions such as, “Which criterion matters most in your claim? Which evidence supports it?” | Analyze data and prepare a claim. |
| 28–35 min | Students write their claim-evidence-tradeoff-limitation explanation. Require a radiation label or explanation where appropriate. | Produce final written explanation. |
| 35–40 min | Conduct a short gallery walk or paired critique. Students identify one evidence-based strength and one question for a peer. | Revise a sentence or add a limitation. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students compare both options against all stated criteria and constraints.
- Students recognize that the lower-cost option may preserve more daylight while the exterior shade may reduce the modeled temperature and cooling index more.
- Students make a defensible choice rather than searching for a hidden “correct” answer.
- Students state that the provided model has limits and name at least one additional kind of evidence they would want.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which numbers in the model support your claim?
- Which option best reduces the modeled desk-surface temperature? Which best preserves daylight? What does the budget allow?
- How does radiation help explain the difference between the current condition and the design options?
- What does this table leave out about a real building or the people using it?

## Safety and Context Boundary

This is a fictional comparison task. Students should not apply the design options to the actual school, interpret building codes, calculate professional costs, or make maintenance recommendations.

## Assessment

Score the final explanation with the full [Heat-Flow Explanation Rubric](../assessments/heat-flow-explanation-rubric.md). Keep exemplary anonymous responses for a later course revisit, if appropriate.

## Adaptations

- Provide sentence frames from the student page.
- Permit students to make an audio or recorded-slide explanation.
- Reduce the matrix to two criteria before adding daylight and cost.
- For extension, ask students to propose what additional data would be needed to test their claim outside the simplified model.
