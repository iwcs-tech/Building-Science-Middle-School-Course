# Lesson 1 Teacher Plan: Notice a Heat-Flow Pattern

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Where might thermal energy be moving in this fictional classroom?

## Learning Target

I can use temperatures and observations to draw a first model of where thermal energy may be moving.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Initial Heat-Flow Model student page](../student/lesson-01-initial-heat-flow-model.md).
- Display Slides 1–4 from the [launch slide outline](../presentations/heat-flow-launch-slide-outline.md).
- Decide whether students will annotate digitally, on paper, or with sticky notes.

## Materials

- Student page, one per learner or group
- Pencils, annotation tools, or sticky notes
- Display or printed scenario data

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 min | Display the fictional classroom scenario. Ask: “What do you notice? What questions do you have?” Record observations without confirming explanations yet. | Notice details and ask questions. |
| 4–8 min | Establish the anchor idea: thermal energy tends to move from warmer to cooler places. Clarify that “cold” is not a substance that flows. | Restate the idea in their own words. |
| 8–18 min | Model how to use one pair of temperatures: the exterior glass is warmer than the interior wall, so an arrow might point from the warmer place toward the cooler place. Do not label mechanisms yet. | Identify relative temperatures and create an initial arrow model. |
| 18–28 min | Ask students to add at least three arrows, two temperature labels, and one note about what evidence led them to an arrow. Circulate and ask, “What is warmer? What is cooler? What evidence supports that path?” | Complete an individual model. |
| 28–35 min | Have pairs compare one similarity and one difference in their models. Invite two or three pairs to share a path they can support with evidence. | Revise or defend part of the model. |
| 35–40 min | Use the exit prompt. Preview that the next meeting will help students name different ways thermal energy can move. | Submit an exit response. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students use the data rather than guessing from the room drawing alone.
- Arrows generally run from relatively warmer to cooler locations.
- Students can distinguish an observation (“the sunny desk is 85°F”) from an inference (“sunlight may be warming the desk”).
- Initial models are expected to be incomplete. Save them for revision in Lesson 2.

## Likely Misconceptions and Useful Responses

| Student idea | Facilitation response |
| --- | --- |
| “Cold moves in through the window.” | “The cooler location matters, but what is actually moving? Try describing thermal energy moving from the warmer place toward the cooler place.” |
| “The sun heats the whole room directly.” | “What surfaces receive sunlight first? Which data points help you trace a more specific path?” |
| “One arrow proves what happened.” | “Our arrows are a model based on limited evidence. What additional evidence would make the explanation stronger?” |

## Safety and Context Boundary

The scenario is fictional. Do not ask students to judge whether their actual classroom has a problem or to recommend facility repairs. Do not collect student comfort or health information.

## Assessment

Collect the initial model and exit prompt. Score informally with the first two rows of the [Heat-Flow Explanation Rubric](../assessments/heat-flow-explanation-rubric.md). Use the results to group students for Lesson 2 support.

## Adaptations

- Provide a partially completed model with one example arrow for students who need a starting point.
- Offer a word bank: warmer, cooler, thermal energy, evidence, model.
- Let students explain arrows orally to a partner or use speech-to-text.
- For extension, ask students to identify a place where the evidence is insufficient to draw a confident arrow.
