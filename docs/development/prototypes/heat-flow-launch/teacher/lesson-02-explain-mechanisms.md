# Lesson 2 Teacher Plan: Explain the Mechanisms

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Which mechanism best explains each simplified heat-flow example?

## Learning Target

I can distinguish conduction, convection, and radiation and use evidence to label a heat-flow model.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Mechanism Sort student page](../student/lesson-02-mechanism-sort.md).
- If using paper, cut the nine evidence cards apart or let student teams use the numbered cards on the page.
- Display Slides 5–8 from the [launch slide outline](../presentations/heat-flow-launch-slide-outline.md).
- Have Lesson 1 models available for revision.

## Materials

- Lesson 2 student page and evidence cards
- Students’ Lesson 1 models
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared digital board

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit the three lesson questions: warmer/cooler, heat path, and evidence. Introduce the names conduction, convection, and radiation with a single visual each. | Connect new terms to the prior model. |
| 5–10 min | Model one card without revealing the rest: identify the evidence, then choose the best-fitting mechanism. State that real situations can involve more than one mechanism. | Follow the reasoning process. |
| 10–22 min | Teams sort the nine cards into three groups and complete the evidence table. Prompt them to disagree with reasons, not guesses. | Sort, discuss, and record evidence. |
| 22–30 min | Review the cards. Ask each team to defend one classification. Emphasize that the cards deliberately simplify the system to highlight a dominant mechanism. | Compare classifications and revise. |
| 30–37 min | Students revise their Lesson 1 model: add mechanism labels to at least two arrows and cite evidence for one label. | Produce a revised annotated model. |
| 37–40 min | Exit prompt: name one mechanism, identify its evidence, and state one place where more evidence would be needed. | Submit exit response. |

## Answer Key for the Simplified Cards

| Card | Dominant mechanism | Reasoning to listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Conduction | Thermal energy transfers through touching materials or connected layers. |
| 4–6 | Convection | Moving air carries thermal energy. |
| 7–9 | Radiation | Energy travels by waves, including sunlight warming a surface. |

## Important Precision

- Do not teach that “heat rises.” Warm **air** may rise because it is less dense, and moving air can carry thermal energy by convection.
- Do not imply that the cards describe every detail of a real building. Building systems often involve multiple mechanisms at the same time.
- Keep the distinction between observation, model, and conclusion visible. The mechanism label is a claim that should be supported by evidence.

## Assessment

Review the sorted table and the revised model with the first three rows of the [Heat-Flow Explanation Rubric](../assessments/heat-flow-explanation-rubric.md). Students who identify a mechanism but cannot cite evidence should receive a brief modeled example before Lesson 3.

## Adaptations

- Start with three cards—one of each mechanism—before adding the remaining six.
- Give selected students a mechanism mat with definitions and icons or shapes, not color alone.
- Let teams record audio explanations of one card instead of writing every justification.
- For extension, ask students to identify a card that could involve a second mechanism in a more realistic system.
