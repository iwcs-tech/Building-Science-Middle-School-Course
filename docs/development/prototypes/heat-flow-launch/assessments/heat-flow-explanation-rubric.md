# Heat-Flow Explanation Rubric

_Draft prototype assessment tool._

Use this rubric for the Lesson 1 initial model, the Lesson 2 revised model, and the Lesson 3 claim-evidence-tradeoff explanation. The final score should be based on the Lesson 3 explanation and supported by the earlier work.

| Criterion | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Extending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Heat-flow model** | Model is missing, unrelated to the evidence, or reverses most warmer-to-cooler relationships. | Shows at least one plausible path, but arrows or temperature relationships need substantial support. | Traces plausible warmer-to-cooler paths and identifies relevant system parts. | Shows multiple connected paths and identifies uncertainty or missing evidence appropriately. |
| **Mechanism and evidence** | Names mechanisms inaccurately or provides no evidence. | Correctly names a mechanism in a simple case but evidence is vague or incomplete. | Correctly uses conduction, convection, or radiation in the relevant simplified case and cites specific evidence. | Explains that a real situation may combine mechanisms while defending the dominant mechanism in the model. |
| **Design reasoning** | Chooses an option without referring to criteria, constraints, or data. | Uses one data point or criterion but does not compare both options clearly. | Makes a defensible recommendation using at least two data points and the stated criteria and constraint. | Weighs competing benefits explicitly and explains why the chosen priority is reasonable. |
| **Tradeoff and limitation** | Does not identify a tradeoff, limitation, or needed evidence. | Names a tradeoff or limitation without explaining its significance. | States one relevant tradeoff and one limitation or additional evidence needed. | Explains how additional evidence could test, revise, or qualify the recommendation. |
| **Communication** | Explanation is difficult to follow or incomplete. | Explanation contains a claim but needs clearer reasoning or vocabulary. | Explanation clearly connects claim, evidence, heat-flow reasoning, and tradeoff. | Explanation is precise, organized, and responsive to a peer question or critique. |

## Suggested Use

- Do not grade the Lesson 1 initial model for accuracy alone; use it to identify prior ideas.
- Give brief feedback after Lesson 2 on the connection between evidence and mechanism labels.
- For Lesson 3, mark one strength and one specific next step. A student can meet the goal with either option if the reasoning matches the provided model.

## Student-Friendly Success Criteria

Before submitting, I can check that I:

- used arrows and temperatures to show a plausible heat-flow path;
- named a heat-transfer mechanism and used evidence;
- compared the design options using data;
- named a tradeoff; and
- explained one limitation or question I still have.
