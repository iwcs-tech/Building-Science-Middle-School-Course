## Three Ways Thermal Energy Can Move

| Mechanism | Student-friendly definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Conduction** | Thermal energy transfers through direct contact between materials. |
| **Convection** | Moving air carries thermal energy. |
| **Radiation** | Energy travels by waves, such as sunlight warming a surface. |

Real building systems can involve more than one mechanism. For this sort, choose the **dominant mechanism** described by each simplified card.

## Evidence Cards

Cut apart the cards if your teacher asks you to, or sort by card number in the table below.

---

### Card 1

A warm indoor wall surface touches a cooler layer inside the wall. Thermal energy transfers across the touching materials.

---

### Card 2

A metal window frame connects a warmer outdoor side to a cooler indoor side. The frame is a continuous material path.

---

### Card 3

During a cold day, a warmer classroom ceiling touches the cooler ceiling materials above it.

---

### Card 4

Air near a warm ceiling becomes warmer than the air lower in the room. The warmer air moves upward and other air moves to replace it.

---

### Card 5

Air next to a cold window cools and moves downward. Other room air moves toward the window area.

---

### Card 6

Air moves through an opening around a door from one part of a building to another, carrying its thermal energy with it.

---

### Card 7

Sunlight crosses an open classroom and warms the top of a desk near the window.

---

### Card 8

A warm surface gives off energy that can travel across a gap and be absorbed by a cooler surface.

---

### Card 9

An exterior shade blocks some sunlight before that sunlight reaches a window surface.
