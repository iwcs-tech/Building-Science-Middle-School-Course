# Feedback-and-Controls Systems Prototype

_Draft instructional prototype for educator review. It is not tested, classroom-ready curriculum._

This two-meeting sequence uses supplied fictional model data to help seventh- and eighth-grade students trace a simplified feedback loop: a changing condition, a monitored signal, a decision rule, and a modeled response. Students then compare fictional control plans using stated criteria and constraints.

## Review Before Use

- Studio Meridian, its dashboard, all readings, rules, responses, and model outputs are fictional instructional evidence. They do not describe the school or a real building-control system.
- Confirm timing, vocabulary, reading level, and print/digital formats with intended learners before classroom use.
- Use the teacher plans as adaptable facilitation notes, not word-for-word scripts.
- Do not ask students to operate thermostats, shades, lighting, ventilation, building-management software, sensors, or other equipment. Do not ask students to inspect the actual school, diagnose conditions, interpret codes, or recommend repairs, purchases, or operating changes.

## Source and Asset Status

EPA describes commissioning as a professional process that verifies whether building systems perform as intended and includes documented operational checks of sensors and controls. This prototype uses that systems idea only as a simplified fictional model; it does not simulate real commissioning or prepare students to conduct it.

- [EPA: Building Commissioning for Schools](https://www.epa.gov/iaq-schools/building-commissioning-part-indoor-air-quality-design-tools-schools)
- [National Academies: Investigation and Design at the Center](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/25216/interactive/2/)
- [NGSS MS-ETS1-1: Engineering Design](https://www.nextgenscience.org/search-standards?keys=MS-ETS1-1)

The fictional cases, dashboard, tables, and prompts are original instructional text. No external visual assets are included.
