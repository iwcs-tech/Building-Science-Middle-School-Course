# Feedback-and-Controls Reasoning Rubric

_Draft review tool for the fictional feedback-loop model and control-plan explanation._

| Criterion | Emerging | Developing | Secure | Extending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Models system relationships** | Names isolated parts or mixes up signal, rule, and response. | Shows part of the sequence but leaves an important relationship unclear. | Accurately traces condition → signal → rule → response → next signal. | Adds a supported feedback connection and clearly bounds what is included. |
| **Uses supplied evidence** | Gives an unsupported opinion or repeats a number without linking it to a claim. | Uses one relevant item of supplied evidence. | Uses relevant table evidence to support arrows or a plan comparison. | Compares evidence across criteria and distinguishes evidence from inference. |
| **Reasons with criteria and constraints** | Chooses a plan without reference to priorities or limits. | Names a criterion or constraint. | Compares plans using stated criteria and constraints. | Explains how competing priorities create a defensible tradeoff. |
| **Qualifies the claim** | Treats the fictional model as proof about a real building. | Names a vague uncertainty. | States a specific limitation or missing information. | Explains why the limitation matters and maintains the fictional-model boundary. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students use “signal” for information in the model, not for the temperature itself.
- Students describe a feedback cycle without claiming it guarantees a result.
- Students can defend either fictional plan when evidence, a tradeoff, and a limitation are present.
- Students do not apply the model to the actual school or propose a real-system action.
