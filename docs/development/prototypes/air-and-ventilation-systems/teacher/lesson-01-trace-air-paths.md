# Lesson 1 Teacher Plan: Trace Air Paths

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> What evidence helps us trace air through this fictional space?

## Learning Target

I can draw a supported model of how air may move through a fictional space and state what I still do not know.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Air-Path Model student page](../student/lesson-01-air-path-model.md).
- Display Slides 1–5 from the [slide outline](../presentations/air-and-ventilation-slide-outline.md).
- Decide whether students work in pairs or groups of three.

## Materials

- Lesson 1 student page
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared digital board
- Optional sticky notes for the “evidence” and “unknown” labels

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Display the fictional Learning Studio Kestrel diagram. Ask, “Where might air enter, move, or leave?” Do not confirm a complete path yet. | Notice and wonder about the diagram. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the three terms: planned ventilation, filtration, and unplanned air movement. Clarify that filtration is not another word for ventilation. | Add the terms to a word bank. |
| 10–18 min | Model one careful arrow: air enters through the marked outdoor-air path because the diagram says the intake is open. Model an “unknown” label for the unmarked wall gap. | Identify evidence and uncertainty in the supplied diagram. |
| 18–28 min | Pairs complete the air-path model. Prompt them to label each arrow as planned, filtered, unplanned, or unknown only when evidence supports the label. | Draw and label the model. |
| 28–35 min | Compare two models. Ask which arrows are observations, which are inferences, and which need more information. | Revise one arrow or uncertainty note. |
| 35–40 min | Exit check: explain one difference between ventilation and filtration, and name one unknown in the fictional system. | Submit a brief explanation. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students use arrows only where the supplied evidence supports a possible path.
- Students identify outdoor-air entry and return/exhaust as a planned path rather than calling every air movement ventilation.
- Students state that a filter can affect air passing through it but does not by itself establish outdoor-air exchange.
- Students name uncertainty instead of assuming a gap or symbol proves a real problem.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which detail in the diagram supports that arrow?
- Is this route planned, filtered, unplanned, or still unknown? What makes you say that?
- Could air pass through a filter without being outdoor air? Could outdoor air enter without passing through the marked filter in this simplified model?
- What would we need to know before making a stronger claim?

## Safety and Context Boundary

Use the fictional Learning Studio Kestrel only. Do not ask students to locate vents, gaps, filters, or air-quality concerns in the actual school or at home. Do not collect health information, enter mechanical spaces, operate equipment, interpret codes, or recommend changes.

## Assessment

Review the model and exit check using the first three criteria in the [Air-and-Ventilation Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/air-and-ventilation-reasoning-rubric.md).

## Adaptations

- Provide an air-path diagram with one planned arrow already completed.
- Read the diagram notes aloud and let students use movable text labels.
- Reduce the task to one planned route and one unknown route.
- For extension, ask students to draw a second model that could also fit the evidence and explain what new evidence would distinguish them.
