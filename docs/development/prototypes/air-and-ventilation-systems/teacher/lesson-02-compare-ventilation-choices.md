# Lesson 2 Teacher Plan: Compare Ventilation Choices

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Which fictional plan best fits the stated priorities, and what is its limitation?

## Learning Target

I can compare fictional air-system choices using supplied evidence, criteria, and constraints, then make a qualified claim.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Ventilation Choice Comparison student page](../student/lesson-02-ventilation-choice-comparison.md).
- Display Slides 6–11 from the [slide outline](../presentations/air-and-ventilation-slide-outline.md).
- Have students' Lesson 1 air-path models available for reference.

## Materials

- Lesson 2 student page and fictional choice cards
- Students' Lesson 1 models
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared digital board

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit Lesson 1: planned ventilation, filtration, unplanned air movement, and unknown. Ask students to correct the statement “a filter is the same thing as ventilation.” | Recall and revise vocabulary. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the fictional priorities and constraint. Model reading one output as evidence, not as a universal rule. | Underline the criteria and constraint. |
| 10–20 min | Teams compare Plan North and Plan South with the evidence table. Require one advantage and one limitation for each plan. | Complete the comparison table. |
| 20–28 min | Discuss competing claims. Emphasize that a choice can meet one priority while creating a tradeoff; neither plan is a real-school recommendation. | Check evidence and revise reasoning. |
| 28–36 min | Students independently write a final claim naming a preferred fictional plan, two evidence details, a tradeoff, and a needed information. | Complete the explanation. |
| 36–40 min | Partners use the success criteria to ask one question that helps qualify a claim. | Give feedback and revise. |

## Suggested Interpretation of the Fictional Models

There is no required “best” plan. Listen for claims that accurately connect the stated priorities to the supplied outputs. Plan North has a marked outdoor-air path through a filter but uses more model energy units. Plan South has greater modeled outdoor-air exchange through open windows but allows more outside-noise units and provides no filter-path value in this simplified comparison. Neither plan establishes what should happen in a real room.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which priority does your evidence address?
- What can this model output support? What does it leave out?
- Does the plan include outdoor-air exchange, filtration, both, or neither in this simplified case?
- What tradeoff makes the decision less simple?
- How can you revise a claim so it is about Learning Studio Kestrel, not every building?

## Safety and Context Boundary

Students compare fictional operating plans only. Do not ask them to judge classroom ventilation, open or close windows for the activity, change a system setting, collect symptoms, inspect equipment, interpret codes, or advise facilities staff.

## Assessment

Score the final explanation with the full [Air-and-Ventilation Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/air-and-ventilation-reasoning-rubric.md). A student may reach the goal with either plan if the explanation uses supplied evidence and names a meaningful tradeoff and limitation.

## Adaptations

- Compare one criterion at a time before students write a full claim.
- Provide sentence frames for claim, evidence, tradeoff, and limitation.
- Let students make an audio or short recorded-slide explanation instead of writing by hand.
- For extension, ask students to propose one additional fictional model output that would make the comparison fairer.
