# Air-and-Ventilation Reasoning Rubric

_Draft prototype assessment tool._

Use this rubric for the Lesson 1 air-path model and Lesson 2 explanation. The final score should be based on Lesson 2 and informed by Lesson 1 work.

| Criterion | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Extending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Air-path evidence** | Draws an unsupported route or leaves the model incomplete. | Identifies one route but does not connect it clearly to supplied evidence. | Draws a plausible planned path and identifies an unknown using supplied evidence. | Compares more than one plausible model and explains what evidence would distinguish them. |
| **Key distinctions** | Treats ventilation, filtration, and unplanned air movement as the same idea. | Names terms but applies one inaccurately or without evidence. | Correctly distinguishes planned ventilation, filtration, and unplanned or unknown movement in the fictional case. | Explains how the ideas can be related without treating any one label as proof of the others. |
| **Comparison with criteria** | Chooses a plan without using the stated criteria or constraint. | Cites one output but does not compare plans clearly. | Uses at least two supplied outputs to compare the fictional plans against stated priorities or the energy constraint. | Weighs several criteria and explains why reasonable people could prioritize them differently. |
| **Tradeoff and limitation** | Gives no tradeoff, limitation, or needed information. | Names one without explaining why it matters. | States a relevant tradeoff and a limitation or additional information needed. | Explains how added evidence could qualify or revise the claim. |
| **Communication and boundary** | Response is incomplete, unclear, or treats the case as a real-building judgment. | Claim is present but needs support with vocabulary, evidence, or the fictional boundary. | Clear claim connects evidence, comparison, tradeoff, and the fictional-case boundary. | Uses precise language and constructively responds to a peer question about uncertainty. |

## Student-Friendly Success Criteria

Before submitting, I can check that I:

- labeled an air path with evidence;
- explained how ventilation and filtration differ;
- compared supplied model outputs against the stated priorities;
- named a tradeoff; and
- stated a limitation or question without making a claim about a real building.
