# Air and Ventilation Systems: Unit Overview

_Draft prototype — two 40-minute meetings._

## Purpose

Students use supplied fictional evidence to trace how air can move through a bounded learning space. They distinguish planned ventilation, filtration, and unplanned air movement, then compare fictional operating choices without treating any choice as a real-school recommendation.

## Candidate Driving Question

> How can air move through a building space, and how can a planned air path involve tradeoffs?

## Candidate Learning Outcomes

Students will be able to:

- draw a supported air-path model using supplied evidence;
- distinguish planned ventilation, filtration, and unplanned air movement;
- identify inputs, outputs, and relevant system parts in a fictional air-system model;
- compare supplied choices against stated criteria and constraints; and
- make a qualified claim that includes a tradeoff, limitation, or needed information.

## Sequence at a Glance

| Meeting | Question | Main student product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Trace | What evidence helps us trace air through this fictional space? | Labeled air-path model with an uncertainty note |
| 2. Compare | Which fictional plan best fits these stated priorities, and what is its limitation? | Claim-evidence-tradeoff-limitation explanation |

## Shared Reasoning Routine

1. Bound the fictional system.
2. Mark where air enters, moves, leaves, or has an unknown path.
3. Name the evidence that supports each arrow.
4. Distinguish planned ventilation, filtration, and unplanned air movement.
5. Compare a stated choice using criteria and constraints.
6. Name a tradeoff, limitation, or information still needed.

## Materials

- Teacher display and student laptops, or printed student pages
- Pencils, highlighters, or digital annotation tools
- Optional chart paper or shared digital board

No sensors, field trip, mechanical-room access, construction tools, measurements of the actual school, or operation of ventilation equipment are required.

## Vocabulary

- **air path:** a route air may follow into, through, or out of a space
- **ventilation:** a planned exchange of air between indoors and outdoors
- **filtration:** passing air through material that captures some particles; it is not the same as ventilation
- **unplanned air movement:** air moving through an unintended opening or route
- **supply:** the route that brings air into a fictional system model
- **return or exhaust:** a route that carries air away from a fictional space
- **criterion:** a feature a choice should achieve
- **constraint:** a limit a choice must stay within
- **tradeoff:** a benefit gained while accepting a cost, limitation, or reduced benefit elsewhere

## Science Background for the Teacher

EPA explains that school HVAC systems are intended to help maintain indoor air quality through adequate ventilation with filtration and to provide thermal comfort. Real systems are complex: outdoor conditions, operation, maintenance, equipment design, and many other factors matter. This prototype deliberately narrows that complexity. Students reason only from supplied fictional diagrams and modeled outputs; they do not evaluate, diagnose, or prescribe action for a real system.

- [EPA: Heating, Ventilation and Air-Conditioning Systems](https://www.epa.gov/iaq-schools/heating-ventilation-and-air-conditioning-systems-part-indoor-air-quality-design-tools)

## Assessment Evidence

Use the [Air-and-Ventilation Reasoning Rubric](assessments/air-and-ventilation-reasoning-rubric.md) to review the Lesson 1 model and Lesson 2 explanation. Prioritize evidence, accurate distinctions, and appropriate uncertainty rather than a single “best” answer.

## Accessibility and Participation

- Provide diagrams in text-and-arrow form; do not rely on color alone.
- Preview vocabulary with a visible word bank and optional icon support.
- Let students draw, type, dictate, or explain their model aloud.
- Assign partner roles such as diagram reader, evidence finder, arrow checker, and questioner.
- Offer a partially completed model or comparison table when needed.

## Prototype Review Questions

- Do students distinguish planned ventilation from unplanned air movement without treating either as a diagnosis?
- Can students explain that filtration and ventilation are related but not identical?
- Does the routine connect naturally to the existing heat-flow and moisture-pathways work?
- Does the fictional choice comparison make criteria, constraints, tradeoffs, and uncertainty visible within 40 minutes?
