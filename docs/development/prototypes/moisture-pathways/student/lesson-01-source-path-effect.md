## Question

> What source, path, and effect can we model from this evidence?

## Important Idea

A moisture model connects:

- a **source**: where water or water vapor may begin;
- a **path**: a route it may take; and
- an **effect**: a change we observe after it reaches a place or material.

Your model is a supported possibility. It is **not** proof of a building problem or a repair recommendation.

## Three Simplified Pathway Labels

| Label | Student-friendly meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Liquid water** | Rain, meltwater, or other liquid water moves along a route. |
| **Water carried with moving air** | Air moves through an opening while carrying water vapor. |
| **Condensation** | Water vapor changes into liquid droplets on a colder surface. |

## Fictional Case Cards

Your teacher will assign one case. These are invented for learning; they do not describe your school.

### Case A: Rainy-Day Ceiling Mark

After a day of heavy rain, a small brown mark is visible on a ceiling tile below the edge of a flat roof. The next morning, the tile feels dry. No one saw where water first entered.

### Case B: Cold Window Droplets

On a cold morning, small droplets are visible on the indoor side of a window. The classroom air feels warmer than the window glass. The room was occupied the previous afternoon.

### Case C: Ceiling-Hatch Air Gap

In a fictional attic room, warm indoor air is shown moving upward through a small gap around a ceiling hatch. Above the hatch is a colder space. The case gives no information about whether droplets are present.
