# Lesson 1 Teacher Plan: Trace a Water Pathway

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> What source, path, and effect can we model from this evidence?

## Learning Target

I can use observations to model a possible moisture source, path, and effect without claiming more than the evidence shows.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Source, Path, Effect student page](../student/lesson-01-source-path-effect.md).
- Display Slides 1–5 from the [slide outline](../presentations/moisture-pathways-slide-outline.md).
- Choose whether teams will annotate digitally, on paper, or on a shared board.

## Materials

- Lesson 1 student page, one per learner or team
- Pencils, highlighters, or annotation tools
- Display or printed fictional case cards

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 min | Display the three fictional observations without pathway labels. Ask: “What do you notice? What questions do you have?” Record observations separately from ideas about causes. | Notice and wonder. |
| 4–9 min | Introduce the source → path → effect routine. Define moisture as liquid water or water vapor in air. Explain that a model is a supported possibility, not proof. | Restate the routine in their own words. |
| 9–14 min | Model one short example: rain is the source, a visible route is the path, and a wet ceiling tile is the effect. Circle the evidence and name one thing the evidence cannot establish. | Follow the difference between evidence and inference. |
| 14–27 min | Assign groups one of the three cases. Prompt: “What exactly is observed? Where might moisture begin? What route could connect the source to the effect?” | Complete a source → path → effect model. |
| 27–34 min | Form mixed groups or conduct a gallery walk. Ask each group to share one arrow and one limitation. Introduce the three simplified labels: liquid water, water carried with moving air, and condensation. | Compare cases and revise labels. |
| 34–40 min | Students complete the exit prompt. Preview that Lesson 2 will ask them to defend a claim from a new case. | Submit a supported pathway claim and question. |

## Suggested Responses for the Simplified Cases

| Case | Plausible pathway label | Reasoning to listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: Rainy-day ceiling mark | Liquid water | Rain or meltwater could enter above the ceiling and follow a route to the observed mark. The evidence does not identify the exact entry point. |
| B: Cold window droplets | Condensation | Water vapor in indoor air can change into droplets on a cold window surface. The evidence does not establish indoor humidity or every cause of the cold surface. |
| C: Air gap near a ceiling hatch | Water carried with moving air | Warm indoor air can carry water vapor through an opening; the path may lead toward a colder surface. The evidence does not prove how much moisture moved or whether droplets formed. |

## Important Precision

- The labels are learning tools, not a diagnostic system. A real situation can include more than one moisture pathway.
- Do not equate a stain, odor, or droplet with a particular cause. Ask, “What does the evidence show, and what would we still need to know?”
- Do not tell students that all visible moisture is dangerous, or invite discussion of personal health symptoms.
- A cold surface can be relevant to condensation; connect this to the course's heat-flow thread only as a possible explanation, not a prerequisite.

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students distinguish what the case states from what they infer.
- Arrows connect a plausible source and effect rather than merely naming a building part.
- Students use pathway labels as claims supported by details.
- Students can name at least one uncertainty or needed information.

## Safety and Context Boundary

All cases are fictional. Do not ask students to inspect, photograph, report on, or diagnose conditions in the actual school or at home. Do not collect health information, test for mold, or give repair instructions.

## Assessment

Collect the annotated model and exit response. Use the first three rows of the [Moisture Pathways Rubric](../assessments/moisture-pathways-rubric.md) for brief feedback before Lesson 2.

## Adaptations

- Give selected students a model with the observed effect already filled in.
- Use sentence frames: “I observe ___. A possible source is ___. It could travel by ___. I still need to know ___.”
- Let students orally record the model rather than write it.
- For extension, ask students to identify a second plausible pathway and explain why the evidence is insufficient to choose between them.
