# Lesson 2 Teacher Plan: Defend a Moisture Explanation

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Which moisture pathway best explains this fictional case, and what information is missing?

## Learning Target

I can make a claim about a moisture pathway, support it with evidence, and explain a limitation.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Moisture Case Analysis student page](../student/lesson-02-moisture-case-analysis.md).
- If using paper, cut apart the three case cards or let groups use the numbered cards on the page.
- Display Slides 6–10 from the [slide outline](../presentations/moisture-pathways-slide-outline.md).
- Have students' Lesson 1 models available for reference.

## Materials

- Lesson 2 student page and fictional case cards
- Students' Lesson 1 models
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared digital board

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit the source → path → effect routine. Ask students to name the difference between an observation and an inference. | Recall Lesson 1 learning. |
| 5–10 min | Model how to analyze one evidence detail: mark what it supports, then name what it does not establish. Do not identify a final answer for the class case. | Practice using evidence cautiously. |
| 10–21 min | Teams analyze the three new fictional cards, choosing the best simplified pathway label and recording evidence and a limitation for each. | Sort and defend classifications. |
| 21–28 min | Discuss one card at a time. Invite competing claims when both identify evidence and limitations. Emphasize that the task is to reason from a simplified case, not to find a hidden professional diagnosis. | Compare and revise thinking. |
| 28–36 min | Students independently complete the final Case Delta explanation: claim, two evidence details, source → path → effect model, and one needed information. | Write an evidence-based explanation. |
| 36–40 min | Conduct a paired check using the success criteria. Invite students to revise one sentence that overstates certainty. | Give feedback and revise. |

## Suggested Classifications for Simplified Cards

| Card | Best label | Reasoning to listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1: Rain after a storm | Liquid water | The stated rain and route near the roof edge support a liquid-water pathway; the exact opening is unknown. |
| 2: Droplets on a cold pipe | Condensation | Warm indoor air and a cold pipe can support water vapor changing to droplets; the case does not establish humidity level or the pipe's full temperature history. |
| 3: Air movement through a gap | Water carried with moving air | Air movement through the gap can transport water vapor; the case does not prove condensation or damage. |

## Facilitation Questions

- Which words are observations? Which words are your inference?
- What source, path, and effect does your model connect?
- Could more than one pathway occur in a real building? Why do we choose one label here?
- What single piece of information would make your explanation stronger?
- Does your claim accidentally sound like a diagnosis? How can you revise it to state a supported possibility instead?

## Safety and Context Boundary

This is a fictional reasoning task. Students should not use these cards to diagnose their homes or school, recommend maintenance changes, interpret codes, or discuss health symptoms.

## Assessment

Score the final explanation with the full [Moisture Pathways Rubric](../assessments/moisture-pathways-rubric.md). A student can meet the goal with a different plausible claim if it accurately uses the provided evidence and names a meaningful limitation.

## Adaptations

- Let students analyze two cards before attempting the final case.
- Provide the source → path → effect frame with one box prefilled.
- Permit an audio explanation or a short recorded slide in place of handwriting.
- For extension, ask students to write a second competing model and compare the evidence needed to distinguish the two.
