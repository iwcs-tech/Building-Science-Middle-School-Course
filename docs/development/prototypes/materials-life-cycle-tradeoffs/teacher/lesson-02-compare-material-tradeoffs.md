# Lesson 2 Teacher Plan: Compare Material Tradeoffs

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Which fictional option best fits the stated goals, and what tradeoff remains?

## Learning Target

I can compare fictional material options using evidence from more than one life-cycle stage and name a tradeoff or limitation.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Material Tradeoff Comparison student page](../student/lesson-02-material-tradeoff-comparison.md).
- Display Slides 5–8 from the [slide outline](../presentations/materials-life-cycle-slide-outline.md).
- Remind students that each rating is invented for learning and cannot stand in for real product research.

## Materials

- Lesson 2 student page
- Students' Lesson 1 journey maps
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared decision matrix

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit the four stages. Ask why a single “eco score” could hide important information. | Identify one stage that might be overlooked. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the fictional reading-room panel brief, criteria, constraint, and table. Model how to compare one stage. | Underline the criteria and identify what each rating represents. |
| 10–20 min | Pairs complete the decision matrix, requiring evidence from at least two stages. | Compare both options. |
| 20–30 min | Students write a claim, evidence, tradeoff, and limitation. Circulate with questions about cost and stage-specific evidence. | Prepare final reasoning. |
| 30–36 min | Conduct a short peer check. Invite more than one defensible choice if the evidence and priorities are clear. | Give a peer one strength and one question. |
| 36–40 min | Students revise one sentence to make the limitation more specific. | Submit the explanation. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students compare at least two life-cycle stages instead of choosing from one rating.
- Students use the stated three-credit constraint.
- Students distinguish a fictional model from a real material recommendation.
- Students can defend different choices when priorities differ and reasoning uses supplied evidence.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which evidence comes from an early stage? Which comes from use or end-of-use?
- What does a high durability rating tell us? What does it not tell us?
- Can an option be stronger in one stage and weaker in another?
- What would a real decision-maker need beyond this fictional table?

## Safety and Context Boundary

This is a fictional comparison task. Students should not research, test, purchase, recommend, or remove actual building materials, nor interpret safety or building standards.

## Assessment

Score the final explanation using the [Materials Tradeoff Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/materials-tradeoff-reasoning-rubric.md). Keep examples that show careful uncertainty, not only a particular option choice.

## Adaptations

- Cover two columns at first, then reveal the remaining stages after a preliminary comparison.
- Provide a partially completed decision matrix with one example note.
- Permit a recorded explanation or an oral conference response.
- For extension, ask students what fictional test or data source could make the comparison more trustworthy.
