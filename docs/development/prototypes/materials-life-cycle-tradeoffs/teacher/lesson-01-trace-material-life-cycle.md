# Lesson 1 Teacher Plan: Trace a Material Life Cycle

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> What happens to a material before, during, and after its use in a building?

## Learning Target

I can sequence a material life cycle and explain why more than one stage matters.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Material Journey Map student page](../student/lesson-01-material-journey-map.md).
- Display Slides 1–4 from the [slide outline](../presentations/materials-life-cycle-slide-outline.md).
- Decide whether students will cut and sort the journey cards or number the stages on screen.

## Materials

- Lesson 1 student page
- Pencils, annotation tools, or optional scissors
- Optional chart paper or a shared digital board

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Show an everyday object and ask: “When does its story begin and end?” Avoid asking students to judge it good or bad. | Offer a first idea about the object's journey. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the four simplified life-cycle stages. Explain that real systems are more detailed, but the model helps us ask better questions. | Record the stage names and definitions. |
| 10–18 min | Model sequencing one fictional journey-card set. Name one question the card raises without supplying a final judgment. | Sequence the remaining cards. |
| 18–28 min | Pairs complete the journey map and identify one possible effect or question for each stage. | Build a four-stage model. |
| 28–35 min | Compare maps. Ask: “What could happen if we only looked at the installation stage?” | Revise or add one stage question. |
| 35–40 min | Exit check: explain why one stage alone may not settle a material choice. | Submit a brief explanation. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students can sequence the stages without needing professional manufacturing knowledge.
- Students identify questions rather than unsupported environmental claims.
- Students recognize that use, maintenance, and end-of-use management can matter alongside extraction and manufacture.
- Students can state that the simplified model leaves out details.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which event happens before this material is installed? Which could happen after it is removed?
- What question could we ask at this stage?
- Why might a durable material still involve a tradeoff? Why might a reusable material still involve a tradeoff?
- What would we need to know before making a real purchase decision?

## Safety and Context Boundary

Use fictional components and invented journey cards only. Do not ask students to test, identify, handle, rate, or recommend actual school materials.

## Assessment

Review the journey map and exit check using the first two criteria in the [Materials Tradeoff Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/materials-tradeoff-reasoning-rubric.md).

## Adaptations

- Start with three stages and add end-of-use management after students establish the sequence.
- Read cards aloud or provide audio support.
- Use matching labels and arrows rather than cut cards.
- For extension, ask students to show how reuse could make a journey loop back to a use stage.
