# Materials Life-Cycle Tradeoffs: Slide Outline

_Draft presentation content. Build an editable slide deck only if it improves delivery for the teacher._

Use simple diagrams, large text, and descriptive alt text. Do not use color as the only way to identify a life-cycle stage, option, or rating.

## Lesson 1: Trace a Material Life Cycle

| Slide | Purpose | Suggested content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Title | Launch the sequence. | “What questions should we ask before calling a material choice better?” Include the driving question. |
| 2. Material journey | Introduce the whole-system idea. | Four arrows: resource acquisition → manufacture → use and maintenance → end-of-use management. State that the model is simplified. |
| 3. Stage questions | Make analysis concrete. | One nonjudgmental question beside each stage, such as “What resources are needed?” or “What could happen after removal?” |
| 4. Your job | Clarify the product and boundary. | “Trace the journey and ask questions. Do not rate real products.” |

## Lesson 2: Compare Material Tradeoffs

| Slide | Purpose | Suggested content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5. Revisit | Connect to Lesson 1. | Ask: “Why can one rating not tell the whole material story?” |
| 6. Design brief | Frame the fictional comparison. | Reading-nook panel, five criteria, and a three-credit limit. |
| 7. Fictional option data | Make the table readable. | Recreate both option rows. Add: “Instructional ratings only; not product advice.” |
| 8. Make and qualify a claim | Support the written explanation. | Sentence frame: “I recommend __ because __. Evidence from two stages is __. A tradeoff is __. A limitation is __.” |
| 9. Reusable routine | Reinforce the thread. | Name stage; identify question; compare evidence; make claim; qualify it. |

## Accessibility Check Before Use

- Each stage name and rating direction appears as text.
- The table says whether higher or lower is better for each column.
- Definitions are visible before students begin analysis.
- Slides use large, readable fonts and sufficient contrast.
