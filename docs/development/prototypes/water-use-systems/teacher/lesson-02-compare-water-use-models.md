# Lesson 2 Teacher Plan: Compare Water-Use Models

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> Which fictional water-use model better fits the stated goals, and what does the model leave out?

## Learning Target

I can compare fictional model outputs using evidence and state a limitation without recommending a real building change.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Water-Use Model Comparison student page](../student/lesson-02-water-use-model-comparison.md).
- Display Slides 6–12 from the [slide outline](../presentations/water-use-systems-slide-outline.md).
- Decide whether students compare models in pairs before writing individually.

## Materials

- Lesson 2 student page
- Lesson 1 maps, if available
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared digital board

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit Lesson 1: “What makes a water-use model supported but incomplete?” | Recall an input, use, output, and unknown. |
| 5–10 min | Present the fictional design brief and distinguish a modeled comparison from an operating recommendation. | Highlight the criteria and constraint. |
| 10–18 min | Model how to read one row: compare the outdoor-use value to the stated criterion, then name the weather-data limitation. | Annotate the two model tables. |
| 18–28 min | Pairs complete the evidence table and decide which fictional model best fits the stated priorities. | Compare outputs and draft a claim. |
| 28–35 min | Invite pairs with different claims to explain which criterion they prioritized. Ask, “What cannot be concluded from these modeled numbers?” | Revise the claim, tradeoff, or limitation. |
| 35–40 min | Individual exit response: claim, two evidence points, one tradeoff, and one needed question. | Submit a qualified explanation. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students use at least two supplied values rather than choosing by preference.
- Students recognize that a lower value in one category may come with a different limitation or evidence gap.
- Students identify the weather-data note and missing information as limits on the comparison.
- Students write about the fictional models only and do not advise their school to change a practice, fixture, or system.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which stated criterion does this value help us evaluate?
- Does a smaller number automatically settle the comparison? What else is in the brief?
- Which facts are modeled outputs, and which facts are missing?
- How could two reasonable readers prioritize the same fictional data differently?

## Safety and Context Boundary

The values and models are fictional. Do not ask students to compare their school's utility use, observe restrooms or equipment, report a suspected leak, estimate a bill, or recommend repairs, purchases, policies, or operating changes. Real water-management decisions belong to responsible adults and qualified professionals with appropriate information.

## Assessment

Score the final explanation with the [Water-Use Systems Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/water-use-systems-rubric.md). The Lesson 1 map can inform feedback on system boundaries and evidence use.

## Adaptations

- Give students a highlighted version of one value from each model.
- Provide sentence frames for claim, evidence, tradeoff, and limitation.
- Let students record an oral explanation before writing.
- For extension, have students add one new fictional data point that would make their claim stronger without turning it into a real-school recommendation.
