# Teacher Plan 1: Trace Home Water Paths

_Review-only draft; 40 minutes; grades 7–8._

## Prepare

Display or distribute the [Home Cedar student page](../student/lesson-01-home-water-map.md). Preview the [unit science notes](../unit-overview.md) and slides 1–4 of the [outline](../presentations/residential-water-slide-outline.md). Define supply, branch, fixture, heater, drain, and system boundary. No actual plumbing or home information is needed.

## Learning target

Students use supplied evidence to trace water into, through, and out of a fictional home, distinguishing planned water use from unwanted enclosure moisture.

| Time | Facilitation move | Student evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Show a simple home outline. Ask where water might enter and leave; label ideas as predictions, not facts about Home Cedar. | Initial path ideas |
| 5–10 min | Read the case aloud. Model one evidence-backed arrow: public supply → service pipe. Explain the boundary. | Case annotations |
| 10–24 min | Students complete the map alone or in pairs. Prompt them to trace cold, hot, drain, and outdoor branches separately. | Labeled map and three evidence notes |
| 24–32 min | Compare two maps. Ask which arrows the card supports, which it does not, and why the garden output is less certain than the sewer output. | Revised map |
| 32–37 min | Contrast the use path with a hypothetical roof leak or condensation; neither is evidenced in this case. | Distinction sentence |
| 37–40 min | Collect the exit response and uncertainty note. | Formative check |

## Look-fors and likely confusions

The water heater is on a branch, not a new external supply. The hot path returns to fixture use and drain. The toilet is a cold-water use in this case. The garden path ends at soil in the evidence; evaporation/runoff amounts are unknown. Students should not infer that a drain always leads to a sewer in every home or that all homes use a public water supply.

## Adaptation and next meeting

Offer a partly labeled text-arrow map for readers who need it. Let students dictate evidence rather than draw. For the next meeting, group students who need a second example of an evidence-backed arrow. Use the [rubric](../assessments/residential-water-reasoning-rubric.md) for formative feedback only.
