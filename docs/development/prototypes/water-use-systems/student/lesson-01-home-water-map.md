# Student Page 1: Map Water in a Fictional Home

_All details describe an invented single-family home. Do not use your own home for this activity._

## Case card: Home Cedar

Home Cedar receives treated water through one service pipe from a public water supply. Inside, a cold-water pipe branches to a kitchen sink, bathroom sink, toilet, clothes washer, outdoor hose tap, and water heater. The heater sends hot water through a separate pipe to the kitchen sink, bathroom sink, shower, and clothes washer. Used indoor water from these fixtures goes through drains to a sewer. Water from the hose tap is used on a small garden and enters soil; this card does not say how much evaporates or runs off. The house also has a roof and walls, but the card gives no evidence of leaks or condensation.

## Make a model

1. Draw a boundary around Home Cedar's water-use system. Place the public supply **outside** the boundary.
2. Draw and label arrows for the incoming supply, cold-water branches, water heater and hot-water branches, indoor uses and drains, and hose-to-garden path. Use words or arrow symbols; color is optional.
3. Next to at least three arrows, quote or paraphrase the case-card evidence that supports them.
4. Circle one water path that is **not** an example of a roof leak or wall condensation. Explain the difference in one sentence.
5. Mark one thing the case card does not tell you. Do not invent a path to fill the gap.

## Exit response

Complete: “In this model, water enters from ___, moves through ___, and leaves the indoor-use part through ___. I cannot tell ___ from this evidence.”
