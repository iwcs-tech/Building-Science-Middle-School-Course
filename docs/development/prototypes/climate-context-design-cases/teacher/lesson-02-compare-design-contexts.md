# Lesson 2 Teacher Plan: Compare a Design in Two Contexts

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> How do the same design options perform differently in two fictional contexts?

## Learning Target

I can use supplied model outputs to compare design choices in two climate contexts and explain a tradeoff or limitation.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Context-Based Design Comparison student page](../student/lesson-02-context-design-comparison.md).
- Display Slides 5–8 from the [slide outline](../presentations/climate-context-slide-outline.md).
- Remind students that the table is fictional model output, not a product comparison or construction recommendation.

## Materials

- Lesson 2 student page
- Students’ Lesson 1 climate cards
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared decision matrix

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit one climate clue from each context. Ask students to predict whether the same option must always win. | Make a prediction and explain it. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the fictional reading-room design brief, criteria, constraint, and model-output table. Model how to read one cell. | Underline the criteria and identify what lower/higher values mean. |
| 10–20 min | Pairs complete the matrix for Pine Crossing, then Sun Mesa. | Compare both options in both contexts. |
| 20–29 min | Ask pairs to prepare a context-based claim. Press for evidence, not a hidden correct answer. | Choose a recommended option for each context or explain why more evidence is needed. |
| 29–35 min | Conduct a paired critique using the peer check. | Identify one strength and one question in another claim. |
| 35–40 min | Students revise their limitation or tradeoff sentence. | Submit final explanation. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students use at least two figures from the appropriate context.
- Students notice that the supplied outputs can favor different options in different contexts.
- Students apply the stated budget constraint.
- Students distinguish a model result from proof about a real building.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which criterion matters most in Pine Crossing? Which matters most in Sun Mesa according to this model?
- What evidence supports your claim in this context?
- Does a higher daylight rating settle the whole decision? Why not?
- What does the table leave out about a real building, materials, people, or cost?

## Safety and Context Boundary

This is a fictional comparison task. Students should not transfer its conclusions to the actual school, calculate a real design, interpret building codes, or recommend repairs.

## Assessment

Score the final explanation using the [Climate-Context Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/climate-context-reasoning-rubric.md). Preserve reasoning that makes different choices in different contexts when both use the provided evidence well.

## Adaptations

- Give students one context first, then add the second after a check-in.
- Provide a partially completed decision matrix.
- Permit a recorded or verbal claim instead of a full written response.
- For extension, have students identify one feature from each option that could be combined in a future fictional model.
