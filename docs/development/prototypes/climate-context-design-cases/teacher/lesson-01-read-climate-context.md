# Lesson 1 Teacher Plan: Read a Climate Context

_Draft prototype — 40 minutes._

## Lesson Question

> What climate evidence matters in this fictional place?

## Learning Target

I can use temperature and precipitation patterns to identify a building-system question worth investigating.

## Before Class

- Share or print the [Climate Context Cards student page](../student/lesson-01-climate-context-cards.md).
- Display Slides 1–4 from the [slide outline](../presentations/climate-context-slide-outline.md).
- Decide whether pairs analyze both cards or begin with one card and then compare.

## Materials

- Lesson 1 student page
- Pencils, annotation tools, or a shared digital board
- Optional sticky notes for evidence sorting

## Timing and Facilitation

| Time | Teacher move | Student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Ask: “Would the same building question matter equally everywhere?” Clarify that weather is short-term while climate describes long-term patterns. | Share an initial idea. |
| 5–10 min | Introduce the two fictional climate cards and model underlining one temperature and one precipitation clue. | Read the cards and annotate evidence. |
| 10–20 min | Pairs complete the evidence map for both places. Prompt: “What does this pattern make you wonder about a building?” | Connect evidence to one heat, water, sunlight, or comfort question. |
| 20–29 min | Collect examples without confirming one correct answer. Sort questions by the system they involve. | Compare questions and revise one explanation. |
| 29–36 min | Introduce the lesson's boundary: a climate card suggests questions; it does not prove a building problem or select a solution. | Add a limitation or question. |
| 36–40 min | Exit check: write one climate pattern and one building-system question it could make relevant. | Submit a brief claim. |

## Teacher Look-Fors

- Students use information from the cards rather than stereotypes about places.
- Students can state that a long-term climate pattern is different from one day's weather.
- Students connect a pattern to a question, not a repair recommendation.
- Students recognize that several building-system questions can be reasonable for the same context.

## Facilitation Questions

- Which words or numbers on the card are evidence?
- What could frequent rain make you want to know about a fictional building? What could hot sunny afternoons make you want to know?
- Does this climate card tell us what is happening in a specific building? Why not?
- What additional evidence would help us compare design choices fairly?

## Safety and Context Boundary

Use fictional places only. Do not ask students to inspect the school, research a real building's failures, interpret a climate zone for construction, or make maintenance recommendations.

## Assessment

Review the evidence map and exit check using the first two criteria in the [Climate-Context Reasoning Rubric](../assessments/climate-context-reasoning-rubric.md).

## Adaptations

- Reduce to one climate card and provide a completed example row.
- Read the cards aloud or offer audio support.
- Let students use icons beside text labels for warm/cool, wetter/drier, and sunnier/cloudier.
- For extension, ask students to identify a way two people might prioritize the same climate evidence differently.
