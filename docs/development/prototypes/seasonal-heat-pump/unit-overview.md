# Seasonal Heat-Pump Transfer Model: Unit Overview

_Phase 1 draft for grades 7–8; two 40-minute meetings._

## Question and candidate learning evidence

How can one home system move heat indoors in winter and outdoors in summer? Students draw a bounded system, label heat and electrical-energy inputs and outputs, explain seasonal direction, and identify a model limitation. This draft does not select equipment, a strand, or an assessment for the course.

| Meeting | Student work | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Trace winter heat and electricity in a fictional home; revise a first model | Labeled winter diagram and explanation |
| 2 | Compare summer and winter, use a simple energy-accounting example | Seasonal diagram, explanation, and limitation |

Use laptops or printed pages, pencils, and teacher display. No equipment, field trip, home information, or special supplies are needed. Offer written, spoken, or drawn responses and labeled arrows rather than color alone.

## Science and model boundaries

An air-source heat pump uses electrical energy to move heat between outdoor air and indoor space. In heating mode it takes heat from outdoor air and releases heat indoors; in cooling mode it takes heat from indoors and releases heat outdoors. A powered refrigeration cycle makes the net transfer from cooler surroundings to warmer surroundings possible; within the equipment, heat transfers locally from warmer to cooler regions. Electricity is an energy input, not a heat arrow from outdoors. The simplified energy accounts in lesson 2 illustrate conservation, not typical performance, measured data, cost, or a forecast. Actual performance depends on conditions and equipment. The model omits refrigerant, fans, defrost, ducts, humidity, and changing temperatures; it does not claim outdoor air is supplied indoors.

Sources: [DOE: Pump Up Your Savings with Heat Pumps](https://www.energy.gov/articles/pump-your-savings-heat-pumps); [DOE: Air-Source Heat Pumps](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/air-source-heat-pumps); [NGSS middle-school engineering design](https://www.nextgenscience.org/topic-arrangement/msengineering-design).

Students do not inspect or change a real system, recommend purchases or settings, or provide household details. Teacher review should check age fit, 40-minute timing, accessibility, and whether the distinction between heat transfer and air movement is clear.
