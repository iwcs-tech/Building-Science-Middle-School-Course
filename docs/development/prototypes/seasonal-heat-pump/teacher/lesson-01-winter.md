# Lesson 1: Where Does Winter Heat Come From?

_Draft facilitation plan, 40 minutes._

**Materials:** [student page](../student/lesson-01-winter.md), display, pencils or laptops. **Look for:** outdoor-to-indoor heat transfer through the powered system, a separate electricity input, and a stated limit.

1. **Notice and predict (5 min):** Present the fictional case. Individually sketch a first idea; accept uncertainty without asking about students' homes.
2. **Read and model (10 min):** Pairs read the evidence card and mark a system boundary, heat arrow, and electrical input. Explain that arrows indicate energy transfer, not a pipe carrying outdoor air into the room.
3. **Discuss (10 min):** Compare drawings. Ask how heat can be taken from cold outdoor air. Air above absolute zero contains thermal energy; the powered cycle makes the overall transfer possible. Avoid claiming heat spontaneously moves from cold to hot.
4. **Revise and explain (10 min):** Students revise in another style or label, cite a card, and name what the diagram leaves out.
5. **Exit (5 min):** Collect the final explanation. Use the [rubric](../assessments/rubric.md) to plan feedback.

Support: read the card aloud and provide pre-labeled indoor/outdoor boxes. Extension: explain why the single large arrow is a simplification. Common misconception: electricity creates all the delivered heat; the model shows heat taken from outdoors plus electrical input. Do not introduce invented numerical efficiency as an observed home measurement.
