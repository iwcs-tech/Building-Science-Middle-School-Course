# Teacher Review and Use Guide

_Draft facilitation notes for educator review—not a teaching script._

## Review Goal

Compare three assessment structures, not student ability or real-building conditions. Keep the same fictional Learning Studio Juniper context across all tasks so the review focuses on the evidence each format produces.

## Suggested Review Session

| Time | Facilitator move | Reviewer work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Revisit the four candidate systems outcomes and the fictional boundary. | Identify the outcome evidence most important for the course. |
| 5–20 min | Share Candidate 1. | Complete or annotate the explanation task. |
| 20–35 min | Share Candidate 2. | Compare the fictional options and mark evidence demands. |
| 35–50 min | Share Candidate 3. | Revise the simple model with supplied data. |
| 50–60 min | Use the rubric and pacing matrix. | Record retain/revise/combine questions; do not finalize a decision in the session. |

## Teacher Look-Fors Across Candidates

- Students distinguish supplied observations or outputs from inferences.
- A system boundary is explicit and does not include unsupported parts.
- An explanation names a relationship rather than only a building part.
- A comparison uses stated criteria and constraints rather than preference.
- A revision changes, adds, or qualifies a model because of supplied evidence.
- Students state what the fictional evidence cannot establish.

## Accessibility and Participation

- Read each table and diagram aloud or provide an accessible digital version.
- Provide a word bank: boundary, input, output, interaction, criterion, constraint, tradeoff, model, revision, and limitation.
- Allow drawing, typing, speech-to-text, oral explanation, or a combination.
- Offer a partially labeled model and sentence frames without removing the evidence-reasoning decision.
- Assign collaborative roles such as evidence finder, model checker, criteria reader, and limitation finder before individual writing.

## Boundary Reminder

Do not substitute a real classroom, utility bill, maintenance observation, student symptom, or facility concern for the supplied materials. If a student raises a real concern, follow the school’s normal adult reporting process; do not turn the task into an investigation.
