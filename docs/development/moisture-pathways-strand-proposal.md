# Moisture Pathways Strand Proposal

_Approved for prototype development; the course-level strand decision remains under educator review._

## Purpose

This proposal develops a grade-appropriate way for seventh- and eighth-grade students to explain how water can enter, move through, or collect in a building. It is intended to support the course's central idea that buildings are interacting systems, without turning students into building inspectors or teaching professional diagnosis.

## Candidate Learning Direction

Students use a **source → path → effect** model to reason from supplied evidence. The prototype focuses on three simplified, observable pathways:

1. **Liquid water:** rain, meltwater, or plumbing water moves along a route.
2. **Water carried with moving air:** air can carry water vapor through an opening.
3. **Condensation:** water vapor can change to liquid droplets on a colder surface.

Real buildings can include several pathways at once. The prototype deliberately does not teach students to select materials, interpret code, diagnose mold, or prescribe repairs.

## Why This Is a Candidate Strand

The DOE Building Science Education materials identify liquid water, air-carried moisture, capillary action, and vapor diffusion as important ways moisture can move through building enclosures. The prototype narrows this professional content to a small, developmentally appropriate explanation routine. It complements—not replaces—the approved heat-flow thread; a colder surface can be one reason condensation occurs.

## Prototype Boundary

- All cases and data are fictional instructional scenarios.
- Students do not examine the actual school, collect health information, test for mold, or enter mechanical or restricted spaces.
- Students make evidence-based models and identify needed information; they do not issue building-maintenance recommendations.

## Source

- U.S. Department of Energy, Building Science Education: [Building Enclosure: Intro Moisture Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/building-enclosure-building-science-intro-moisture-flow).

## Next Review Decision

After educator review or classroom testing, decide whether moisture pathways should become an essential course strand, an optional case within a broader systems unit, or a concept revisited alongside heat flow and air movement.
